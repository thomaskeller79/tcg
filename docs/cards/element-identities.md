# Element Identities — Brainstorm

*Not rules, not decided. A first take on the eight Elements (`PLAN.md` Track C item 1), written by Claude on request, deliberately without leaning on how Magic splits its colors. Not yet discussed: the user has their own picture of all eight, and this is one input to that discussion. The user's earlier notes were worked into it; what was left over (card ideas, mid-level material) is in `idea-backlog.md`.*

**Status:** Brainstorm draft · **Date:** 2026-11-08

**Wheel order** (confirmed by the user, 2026-11-08): D51's list, Light, Fire, Metal, Earth, Darkness, Ice, Water, Air, closing back to Light. Each Element has two neighbours and one direct opposite, and the neighbour/opposite relationships below are confirmed too.

---

## The four axes

Each opposite pair argues about one thing, and each axis is something Leyline already has in the engine:

- **Light vs Darkness: knowledge** — beliefs, observation, Pretends, the hidden Root.
- **Fire vs Ice: time** — traces, Durations, Future, fading.
- **Metal vs Water: form** — permanents, Items, Structures, permanent changes, false forms (Mimics).
- **Earth vs Air: place** — the hex board, terrain, the bond network, Slices.

---

## Light

*Between Air and Fire; opposite Darkness.*

**Philosophy: truth, not goodness.** Light wants everything seen and everyone true to themselves. It doesn't care whether the truth helps anyone.

**Identity: reveals, wins over, restores.**
- **Revealing:** turns deduced values into certain ones, anywhere — on the board, in the Root, cards, traces: reveals the Root, collapses Pretends and Mimics, shows a trace's true content, and makes a card that went anonymous after an unseen draw known again.
- **Winning creatures over:** Light convinces with the truth. It recruits Neutral creatures, frees injured enemies from their controller so they turn Neutral, and wins back deserters (Darkness dominates with fear instead). On the control axis (`PLAN.md` Track C item 1) Light moves creatures up by any number of steps as far as neutral ally, but reaches "you control it" only from neutral ally: every creature Light controls was an ally first. Jumping an indifferent or hostile creature straight to your control skips the convincing — that's compulsion, not Light. Allies are Light's strength: they fight for it without draining its resources, are predictable, and show its growing following on the board; taking the last step is a separate, deliberate choice.
- **Neutral actors:** Light has a general advantage with Neutral permanents. It creates Neutral actors whose Behavior favours Light — powerful early and cheap, since the opponent knows what they'll do — and existing Neutrals lean toward it (e.g. they don't attack Light creatures). Usable often because it isn't broken, unlike taking control.
- **Restoring the true self:** cleanses a creature back to its printed stats, or flickers it, which also sends it back as what it really is.
- **Banners:** Light's Item kind. Clearly visible to everyone, a banner affects all Units in an area around it that follow it. Unlike Fire's musical instruments it needs no Energy, and its effect lasts as long as its conditions hold.

**Creatures: guardians and leaders.** Few, and they stand in front of the Neutral and won-over creatures that make up most of Light's army. Defending is their job, often with Defender. Transcendental: at the start of its controller's turn, all damage is removed from it — they take hits on the opponent's turn and are whole again on yours. Medium Health (enough to hold one turn), low to medium Power, medium Energy, mostly spent defending. Some fly (Air is the neighbour).

**Weaknesses:** burst — a focused attack or Fire's area damage in one turn kills a guardian outright, while slow attrition does nothing until it outpaces the reset; predictable (its Neutral allies' Behavior is known, and its creatures are what they appear to be); poor in the Root.

**Feel:** "I know exactly what you have. And half your army is already listening to me."

**Deck idea — converting control:** Light plays control by turning threats around, not by countering them (countering is Ice's). Revealing shows what's coming, so every answer is exact; cheap Neutral actors and transcendental guardians absorb the first wave; freeing an injured enemy and recruiting it removes a threat and gains a creature at once — Light's card advantage. In the end the opponent's army has become Light's following. Ice controls by stopping things before they happen, on the Aether; Light by turning them after they arrive, on the board and the control axis. Weak against burst turns that kill the guardians first, and against threats that can't be won over (Structures, Items, creatures under Darkness's full control).

**Scenario idea — the fleeing apprentice** (from the user's Light story): the hero sees their master defeated and flees from the horde that did it; unusually, the hero must reach the next landmark themselves. Fleeing Neutral creatures can be taken under control by the hero's charisma — the trick is finding the moment when enough have joined to break through (waiting longer brings more enemies). Later levels: fewer fleeing creatures, but earlier recruits can be summoned, some with "target injured creature loses its controller"; then an enemy whose creatures make the hero's creatures flee or desert, which the hero wins back (originally Darkness's fear; fleeing has no owner yet); later still, bringing light into the dark (revealing) and playing control by converting threats (see the deck idea above).

## Fire

*Between Light and Metal; opposite Ice.*

**Philosophy: everything is fuel.** Nothing should stay as it is; a forest is firewood that hasn't burned yet. Not angry or impulsive: hungry. Keeping anything is a waste.

**Identity: spreads and consumes.**
- **Spreading and escalating:** Fire effects walk from hex to hex over time. A Fire trace sits in Future and returns each turn to reach the next ring. The more Fire catches, the stronger and wider it gets.
- **Consuming its own things** for bursts: a bonded terrain, a permanent it fells, its own traces, cards from the Library. Long-term value turned into one huge turn.
- **Scorched ground:** Fire hits an area around a point, not picked targets (Metal is the precise one). Burning ground hurts whatever stands in it, turn after turn, and burned terrain stops producing mana for a while or for good. Fire changes the map, and its own network is the first thing it eats.
- **Speeding up time:** the mirror of Ice holding traces. Fire makes traces resolve earlier, changes them, and makes lasting effects end sooner by destroying their traces in Past — its own and the opponent's, for the sake of change. It never nullifies a trace before it resolves: that would prevent change. (Ice delays traces in Future, Fire speeds them up; Ice destroys traces before they happen, Fire destroys them after.)
- **Now or never:** what Fire gives is only usable this turn, then it's gone — cards drawn that must be played now, chances that burn out.
- **Pacts:** power now, price later — the mirror of Ice's slow build (pay now, get more later). A creature gains an ability or a boost at once; the drawback waits as a trace in Future and comes due on a fixed turn. Everything is fuel, including your own future.
- **Musical instruments:** Fire's Item kind. A bonus or ability for everyone in an area, usually costing all of the user's Energy.
- **No corpses:** what Fire kills doesn't leave a normal Remnant. Whether it leaves nothing or something else (ash Fire can use) is open.

**Weaknesses:** can't hold ground (its own area becomes ash); spreading doesn't pick sides and burns its own Units; runs dry in a long game.

**Feel:** "I'll light this corner. In three turns it's your problem, and maybe mine too."

**Deck idea:** still open — the look-alike elementals moved to Water, which owns deceit (a starter deck should show what its Element is strongest at). Fire keeps its own disguise as a card-level example: a Mimic on a creature's Summon trace, so it wears off when the trace fades, and burning that trace for a burst gives the disguise up.

**To think about:**
- **Burning the opponent's terrain:** scorching enemy ground, not just its own. Part of the open question of how terrain transformation should work at all.

## Metal

*Between Fire and Earth; opposite Water.*

**Philosophy: things should be made.** Value comes from shaping raw nature into something that lasts and fits a purpose. Patient, plans ahead, trusts what it built over what it found.

**Identity: builds, carries, conducts.**
- **Items and Structures:** Metal Units carry several Items, hand them to each other and get the most out of them.
- **Lasting upgrades:** invests in one permanent over many turns through permanent changes, not temporary buffs. A Metal Unit at turn 8 isn't the card you played.
- **Weapons and armor:** Metal's Item kind. Stays equipped for good and works passively, mostly in combat.
- **Conducting:** Metal objects connect to each other: effects pass along a chain of connected Metal Units and Structures (a Power Grid giving every connected Metal Unit +1 Power; magnetism pulling a connected Metal Unit closer).
- **Precision:** strikes one exact target instead of an area. Lightning is Metal's: the spark runs along what Metal built.
- **Heavy bodies:** high Health, little Energy (Iron Golem with 2 Energy is Metal in spirit); slow on its own, mobile only as part of the machine. Takes force head-on: Armor and toughness.

**Weaknesses:** slow and arrives late; a bare Metal Unit is unimpressive, its strength is its equipment; rigid, can't adapt, and losing the one invested Unit hurts badly; take one link out of a connected group and the rest are stranded; belongs on the Ground (Sky and Root are hard for it); predictable.

**Neighbours:** Fire is the forge, Earth is the ore. Metal is Earth put through Fire.

**Feel:** "Give me five turns and I'll have built something you can't get through."

**Deck idea — rollerblade dwarfs:** slow, steampunk-inspired dwarfs on wheels that use magnetic attraction and repulsion to move efficiently relative to each other. Alone, a dwarf crawls; as a linked group they get around the board well — but the opponent can see where the chain can go, and pulling one link out strands the rest.

**Scenario idea — the Metal campaign** (from the user's Metal story): it starts with a standard deck of creatures and a few typical Metal spells. Against opponents with better creatures one-on-one, ever better armoury spells become necessary (maybe made better by Hero abilities, so a spell levels up — another way to individualise Heroes). Then opponents with mass removal arrive and the starting deck trades badly, so buildings come in: slower, but they win on card advantage. As pressure on the buildings grows, the hero discovers electricity and starts using removal and removal buildings to hold out until the card advantage wins. Last comes an opponent whose onslaught never lets up and who is very hard to beat in their own realm, so a combo must do it: a modest electric tower (short range, lots of damage, ideally an X spell) whose range other cards keep extending (e.g. a creature: "when this enters, target long-range weapon you control in your realm gains 1 distance") until the enemy Hero is in reach — and the combo must be protected on the board against the onslaught.

**To think about:**
- **Bridging the bond network:** Metal Structures keep a path working where it would otherwise be broken (the opposite of Knotting). Unclear when it would matter, since a bond can only extend the network anyway.

## Earth

*Between Metal and Darkness; opposite Air.*

**Philosophy: you are where you stand.** Strength comes from place, not from self. Loyal to ground and ancestry, distrusts anything that drifts, thinks in generations rather than turns.

**Identity: takes the board.** Metal builds on the map; Earth takes the map itself. It wins by moving its home ground forward until the enemy Champion stands in Earth's land.
- **Expanding the network (ramp):** bonds faster and wider than anyone. In Leyline mana comes from bonded terrain, so ramp *is* taking ground. Rewards a wide, healthy network and is best at repairing a cut one. Earth is the honest Element: it pays the real price for its spells, but makes sure it has the resources quickly.
- **Taking terrain:** best at stealing enemy terrain (knot it, then bond it, D122). Every stolen terrain shrinks the enemy's mana and grows Earth's home ground.
- **Strong where rooted:** stronger on its own bonded ground, so every new terrain moves its strength forward. Hard to move: resists displacement. Tough by mass (lots of Health), not by Armor like Metal.
- **Shaping the board:** changes its shape, not what it produces. Walls (as Structures) block movement and cut the enemy army apart; chasms swallow Ground Units into the Root; quicksand traps a Unit that enters it.
- **The Root is its home:** Earth Units burrow (Subterranean), travel unseen and break out where nobody expected, right next to a Champion whose defenders are walled off. Root Units are expensive, which is what the ramp pays for. Its hiding is physical, not magical — it fits the idea that physical actions in the Root go unseen (`PLAN.md` Track A item 69).
- **Striking from its own ground:** area effects like Fire's, but always starting from a terrain in Earth's own realm — an earthquake that breaks Structures or damages Units around that point, or a landslide that shoves everything in an area together, by force and downhill (Water moves things where it wants them, Air clears them away). The further the realm reaches, the further Earth can strike.
- **Runes:** Earth's Item kind. Placed on a hex, with a lasting, possibly hidden effect there. (Needs Items that sit on a hex, not on a Unit.)

**How a game goes:** a slow siege. Each turn the front moves a few hexes, the enemy realm shrinks, and in the end the Champion stands alone on a small island of its own land — and something breaks out of the ground beside it.

**Weaknesses:** slow, a quick opponent wins before the siege closes; ordinary away from bonded ground; no Sky, flyers go over walls and chasms; slow to turn toward a threat across the map.

**Feel:** "You can come into my land. You just won't like how it feels to stand there."

**To think about:**
- **Changing what a terrain is:** turning terrain into another kind, healing scorched ground, earthquakes shifting hexes. Part of the open terrain question.
- **Earth and the dead:** the dead return to the ground and feed it (Circle of Life). A possible Earth angle on Remnants, distinct from Ice raising them unchanged.
- **Who builds which Structures:** Metal builds made things, Earth raises natural ones — settled in the Structure-kind design attempt (`PLAN.md` Track C item 1).

## Darkness

*Between Earth and Ice; opposite Light.*

**Philosophy: until it's seen, it can be anything.** Not evil in itself, though at home in the frightening: an army nobody can count. It values possibility and privacy: what isn't known can't be held against you. Light wants every question answered; Darkness wants every question kept open.

**Identity: keeps things unobserved.** Darkness doesn't make the opponent believe something false (deceit is Water's); it makes them observe nothing at all.
- **Areas of darkness:** covers hexes so nothing inside is observed — the scenario `PLAN.md` Track A item 2 mentions. Darkness hides a place. Inside, the opponent only has deductions.
- **Hidden traces:** the opponent observes nothing of a Darkness trace, either directly or because it's cast under a cloud of darkness. Needed to make everything else here work.
- **Hidden marks** (working name): effects Darkness puts on any permanent, whoever controls it, that help or hurt — and that the opponent doesn't even know exist. No false belief, no observation at all. A weaker relative: observing everything an enemy creature observes, without taking control of it. The extreme, hard to model: taking control by secret compulsion, so the creature still looks like the opponent's until it turns.
- **Breaking knowledge:** makes known cards anonymous again and pushes beliefs from certain back to deduced. It doesn't destroy what the opponent knows; it makes it unreliable.
- **A hidden army:** gathers many creatures of all sizes and roles under its cloud. The threat is that the opponent can't count it: how big it is, what's in it, where it strikes.
- **Ruling by fear:** on the control axis (`PLAN.md` Track C item 1) Darkness takes creatures into its full control, from any state, and never leaves one neutral: a creature ruled by fear would turn back to its old beliefs without the pressure. Sometimes it only severs a creature (it turns hostile to its old controller without joining Darkness). The mirror of Light: Light ends at ally and needs consent for the last step, Darkness skips consent. A card design option, not a must: tying the control to a condition that keeps up the pressure (an upkeep, as long as the dominating creature lives, while the creature stays in darkness, a Duration). Some evil is fine in every Element; Darkness just mustn't be all evil.
- **Tomes:** Darkness's Item kind. A reusable machine that grants abilities: whoever reads it learns what it holds — knowledge comes from books. What a creature learned can stay hidden, so a plain soldier suddenly knows something. Balanced the usual ways (the reading costs Energy or mana, limited uses), a pact is not needed. (Sigils, marks on a creature that grant abilities, were considered and don't fit: knowledge leaves no visible marks. Maybe for another Element.)

**Creatures: an army of specialists.** Plain soldiers in different sizes, plus roles: unseen hunters (stronger while unobserved, the strike force), cloud carriers (take an area of darkness with them as they move, the backbone), watchers (scouts, invisible or seeing through enemy eyes), mark planters (put hidden marks on what they touch). Stats vary by role.

**Weaknesses:** clear the cloud and the army stands exposed — Light reveals, Air clears space; once observed it's an ordinary army the opponent can finally count; hidden marks do nothing until they matter.

**Neighbours:** with Earth it owns the Root (Earth hides beneath, Darkness hides in what isn't observed). With Ice, the stillness of night: Ice stops things, Darkness keeps them unknown.

**Feel:** "You think you know what's in that cloud. Do you want to bet your Champion on it?"

**Deck idea — the army under the cloud:** cloud carriers at the core, moving an area of darkness across the board with the army inside it — soldiers, hunters, watchers and mark planters the opponent can't count or tell apart until they strike.

**To think about:**
- **The dead:** decided in `PLAN.md` Track C item 17 (Remnants per Element).

## Ice

*Between Darkness and Water; opposite Fire.*

**Philosophy: keep the moment.** The present is worth preserving; change is loss. Not cruel: a keeper who doesn't want anything to slip away.

**Identity: stops time.**
- **Freezing Units:** drains Energy and stops recharge. The cleanest "stop" in the game, and on its own it kills nothing.
- **Shatter:** what's frozen becomes fragile and can be destroyed. Ice's own way to kill: first stop it, then break it.
- **Holding traces:** extends Durations, keeps its own traces from fading, delays spells by holding a trace in Future, or stops one for good before it resolves (a counter). The only Element that makes a temporary effect last.
- **Locking values:** a frozen permanent can't change, for better or worse — protects Ice's own investment or freezes an enemy before it grows. Stasis goes further: a permanent is taken out of play, held in the Aether's Future, and comes back exactly as it was.
- **Slow build:** pay now, and the spell waits in the Aether's Future until it's ready, then arrives bigger than its cost. The mirror of Fire's "now or never": later, but bigger. While it waits the opponent sees it coming and can prepare, and Fire can speed it up before it's ready.
- **Crystals:** Ice's Item kind. They charge up over several rounds under certain conditions, then release a big, delayed effect.
- **Preserving the dead:** Ice keeps Remnants intact, and when it raises one it comes back exactly as it died, permanent changes included. By default a raise reads the printed card (`PLAN.md` Track C item 13); Ice would be the exception. Not armies of the dead: *that* one, unchanged. (Would need a rule: a Remnant keeps only name and traits today.)
- **Freezing ground:** puts terrain bonds on hold, makes terrain impassable or passable (frozen water can be walked on).

**Weaknesses:** no growth, falls behind anything that builds up; reactive, needs the opponent to act first; freezing doesn't pick sides; Fire undoes it.

**Neighbours:** Darkness is the stillness of night, Ice its cold. Water is Ice set free: Ice is Water that decided to stop.

**Feel:** "Nothing is going to happen this turn. Or the next. I'll decide when it starts again."

**To think about:**
- **Defensive bulwarks:** ice walls that melt after a while, as Ice's take on walls (Earth's in this draft). Part of the open terrain question.

## Water

*Between Ice and Air; opposite Metal.*

**Philosophy: there's always a way through.** Holding a fixed shape is how you break; giving way is how you survive and in the end win. Earth waits in one place; Water keeps moving until it finds the gap.

**Identity: moves things and wears them down.**
- **Displacement:** pushes, pulls and carries Units — currents, waves, tides, and lure, where the Unit itself moves somewhere it doesn't want to go. Wins by putting things where they don't want to be.
- **Flow around obstacles:** Water doesn't break what's in its way, it gets past it. Water Units move through hexes other Units occupy (they just can't end their move there), and poison damage ignores Armor. It defends the same way: where Metal stops a force head-on, Water turns it aside, sending a spell or ability to another target, or a Unit entering a hex to a different one.
- **Potions:** Water's Item kind. A one-time effect on the Unit that uses it, then it's gone. The opposite of Metal's weapons and armor, which stay equipped for good: Water spends what Metal keeps.
- **Constant dripping wears the stone:** small effects that add up over turns. Poison wears down living Units; erosion strips permanent changes, wears down Structures, rusts Items.
- **Washing back:** Bounce is Water's. Against enemies it's brutal on Metal, whose investment is exactly the permanent changes a Bounce wipes. On its own permanents it's a resource: a biter strikes once and flows back to the Mind, ready to be cast again.
- **Healing as moving damage:** damage persists and healing is rare; Water washes damage off one Unit or shifts it onto another.
- **Deceit:** Water takes false forms: its Units look like something else (Mimics), so its threats may not be what they seem. The opponent believes something false — unlike Darkness, where they observe nothing. Every Element has some disguise in its own way (Fire's fades with its Summon trace); Water's lasts, and it's Water's main strength. Pretends (a false claim, not a false form) sit on the knowledge axis and belong to no Element: a bluffing tool every Element uses its own way.

**Weaknesses:** low Power, rarely kills quickly; nothing it does lasts and its Units don't grow; needs something to push against.

**Neighbours:** Ice is Water that stopped. Air is its lighter cousin: Water moves other things, Air moves itself.

**Feel:** "I don't need to beat your Golem. I'll just put it somewhere it can't matter."

**Deck idea — sirens and biters** (from the user's Water story): small poisonous biters wait hidden in the water; sirens lure the enemy onto them. Each biter bites once, poisons and flows back to the Mind to be cast again, and the poison wears the enemy down. The story map starts as a river delta, where water is everywhere; further upriver the water thins out, enemies stay away from it, and the deck turns to flooding enemy terrain so its creatures (and later big ones like krakens) can attack there. Weak spot: flyers.

**Deck idea — look-alike spirits** (moved from Fire): water spirits that all look like the same basic spirit to the opponent but are each very different (one is a lord for the others, and so on). The opponent can't tell which one to go for. The disguise is a lasting Mimic; it washes off only under the break conditions each card states (D118) — for example when the spirit acts, or when it's revealed. The right break conditions are a playtest question.

**Scenario idea — the delta** (from the user's Water story): the hero lives in a river delta (merfolk or naga) whose river yields ever less food. Part 1: a much stronger Water people upriver takes more and more fish; the hero wants revenge but their tribe is too weak, so they seek allies among the delta's other tribes, each joining once the hero does a task for it — e.g. a single scenario where the hero may only have spells in their deck and must defeat one creature. Everything happens in water-rich land, so Water creatures' drawbacks away from water don't matter yet. Part 2: with allies (Companions?) won, the war upriver begins; the first non-water terrain limits movement, but a big river still links the key places, and the hero must use poison creatures well to beat stronger enemies. It turns out that people only gathers food for a still mightier, non-water empire that kidnapped the princess (?). Part 3, the finale: most terrain is no longer water; it may open with defending against an attack, then turn to the offensive.

**To think about:**
- **Hidden under the surface:** Water Units unseen on water hexes until they attack, which would make the lure-and-poison ambush work. Physical hiding in terrain, like Earth's in the Root.
- **Tied to water:** Water Units strong in and near water, slow or weak away from it.
- **Accumulation:** building up resources over turns (pools, ramp) and releasing them later.

Abilities tied to the terrain a Unit stands on (bonuses or transforming terrain) still need to prove they're fun and work; Magic's experience with such mechanics suggests it isn't simple.

## Air

*Between Water and Light; opposite Earth.*

**Philosophy: belong nowhere.** Owning a place means being tied to it, and anything you're tied to can be taken. Curious and restless, at its best when nobody can say where it'll be next.

**Identity: moves itself.**
- **Flight:** owns the Sky Slice. Flies over walls, chasms, water and guards, and reaches the Champion from directions the map didn't plan for.
- **Lots of Energy:** high Energy, easy recharge. Move, attack, move again — hit and run, never staying where it attacked. A keyword for it: its Attack costs `3En` instead of the default `3~En`, so it can still act after attacking.
- **Escape and teleport:** moves without crossing the hexes in between. Escape is an emergency teleport: leaving combat or a threat instead of resisting it.
- **Clearing space:** pushes enemies far away, not precisely — Water moves others to where it wants them, Air moves them out of the way.
- **Reaching any distance:** Air's effects need no starting point. Earth's must start from its own realm, and most Elements' from near one of their own permanents (a target restriction printed per card, see the card design guidelines in `PLAN.md` Track C).
- **Powder clouds:** Air's Item kind. Thrown, it bursts into a cloud that stays as a zone and affects everyone who crosses it, maybe drifting with the wind.
- **Speed:** acts at faster Speeds. The Element of the first move, not the last word.
- **Free of the network:** cares least about how its terrain connects. Its effects don't need bonded ground, and its Units neither block nor bridge flow. Freedom and weakness in one.

**Weaknesses:** almost no Health; can't guard, block or occupy terrain to choke a bond; little lasting presence if the hit-and-run doesn't finish the game.

**Against Earth:** Earth can't reach the Sky; Air can't break what Earth has rooted. Each is the other's frustration.

**Neighbours:** Water moves other things, Air moves itself. Light is the open sky: Air brings the reach, Light the sight.

**Feel:** "I was never there. And now I'm behind your Champion."

**Deck idea — stormcallers:** Air elementals with the Stormcaller ability: lose 1 Power to send a windblast in a chosen direction, starting from the creature. Every enemy in its way is pushed 1 hex in that direction (maybe taking 1 damage). If the blast hits another stormcaller, that one absorbs the Power and the blast stops — and the absorbed Power is fuel for its own blast. Each blast still costs the firing creature Energy, so the wind can't bounce back and forth forever. The opponent has to read the stormcallers' positions to see where the next blast comes from.

# Decision Log

*Resolved design decisions, newest first. Each: the call, why, and what it touches. Open questions live in the individual docs, not here.*

**Status:** Active · **Date:** 2026-07-23

### D50 — Trace Duration default; Properties/Abilities is card-text vocabulary only
Default Trace Duration: 5 rounds in the Past before a trace vanishes (placeholder, real number deferred to tuning), with `Duration X` as a card-level override — fills D16's previously-unspecified "configurable window" with a concrete shape. Separately: **Properties vs. Abilities** (Properties = plain stat changes like Power/Life/AP; Abilities = named granted capabilities) is adopted as **card-text/readability vocabulary only** — it does not change removability mechanics. The general principle stays "an effect lives as long as its vessel does" (a Trace, or a Creature with a static ability granting it) — the same source-tied Modifier pattern already in `../../architecture/continuous-effects.md`; nothing is ever targeted as an independent "buff object," removal always goes through removing the source. Whether a plain stat buff bakes directly into the stat vs. becomes a full removable ability-object is left an open implementation choice, for playtesting to settle. → `../overview.md` §1, `../economy.md`.

### D49 — Champion/Companion Attack unifies with the generic Actor default
Champion and Companion Attack drops its bespoke figures (previously `5!AP` connected / `3!AP` disconnected for both) and instead **is** the generic Actor default: `3!AP` base, doubled to `6!AP` while network-bonded — the same formula as a plain Creature's Attack (D10) plus the existing bonded-cost-doubling pattern (D9/D22). A real simplification, not renumbering. → `../champions.md`, `../companions.md`.

### D48 — First-player Champion enters with reduced starting AP
New first-move-advantage balancing lever: the Champion of the player who goes first enters play with **reduced** starting AP (placeholder: 4) instead of its full Activation Capacity; the second player's Champion enters with full Activation Capacity as normal. Exact number deferred to tuning. → `../champions.md`, `../economy.md`.

### D47 — Activation Capacity extends to non-Actor Objects, additive to the ownership tree
**"Activation Capacity"** is the formal name for the existing implicit AP-max concept (D10/`../economy.md`) — a pure rename for Actors. **New:** non-Actor Objects (Terrain, Structure, Ruins, Graves) now also carry their own Activation Capacity (default 1) and AP, purely to self-gate "has this printed ability already fired this round" — reusing the existing AP-refresh-at-Beginning-phase mechanism instead of a bespoke once-per-turn clause or a tap/untap subsystem. **Additive** to `../ownership.md`'s funding/payment-walk model, not a replacement: the bonder still pays the mana cost as before; the Object's own AP only answers "already used this round," independent of who funds it. Closes a real gap — nothing previously capped how many times a Terrain/Structure ability could fire per round. Default is once/round (pessimistic default, D14); a specific card may print a higher Activation Capacity for a deliberately repeatable ability. **Objects enter play with full Activation Capacity immediately** (no summoning-sickness delay), unlike an Actor, which still enters at 0 AP (D10/D20) — a pragmatic type-based split, accepted for now; a more general rule (keyed on *what the AP can be spent on* rather than object type) was considered and parked, not adopted. **Graves may carry their own printed activated abilities** under this mechanism — a new, intentional "graveyard matters" design space. → `../economy.md`, `../ownership.md`, `../resources-terrain.md`, `../structures-items.md`.

### D46 — Casting/activating: pay cost before instantiating; legal-target precondition required
**Supersedes D33's ordering.** Playing a card or activating an ability now pays its complete cost **before** choosing targets/modes — reversing D33's target-then-pay order. Necessary for X-costs and cost-scaled modal effects, where available choices depend on what was actually paid. Formalized as two stages: **cost instantiation** (paid cost fixes available values/modes) then **effect instantiation** (remaining choices, incl. targets). **Precondition:** a card/ability is only offered as a legal play if at least one legal cost-and-target combination exists — no paying into a dead cast; matches how legal-command enumeration already has to work. D33's fizzle-on-illegal-target-at-resolution rule is unchanged; only the pay/target order at cast time moves. → `../structures-items.md` (rewrite casting procedure), `../interaction-stack.md`.

### D45 — Speed system (Slow/Quick/Reactive/Instant) replaces D5, D23, and the enumerated priority-window list
**Supersedes D5** (card-level instant/sorcery) **and D23** (`^` per-ability marker); retires `../interaction-stack.md`'s enumerated "priority opens at these events" list. Every card/ability has one of four speeds, gated by the live contents of the Aether's Pending region:
- **Slow** — controller's main phase only, and only while Pending is empty. (≈ today's sorcery-speed.)
- **Quick** — whenever only **Physical Traces** are in Pending. A Physical Trace is the trace left by Move, Attack, Ascend, or Descend (confirmed set; extensible). Physical Traces still enter Pending (respondable — a real priority window exists) but on resolution are **removed from the Aether entirely, zero Past residency** — bypasses Trace Duration (D50) rather than using a short number, keeping the Past meaningful despite how often these fire.
- **Reactive** — whenever no **Instant Trace** is in Pending. Kept even though currently vestigial (see Instant) — a robustness safeguard for a possible future card that makes an Instant linger.
- **Instant** — playable any time, and **resolves fully atomically**: enters Pending and Now immediately advances past it as part of the same action — zero duration, no response window for anyone, not even another Instant. Deliberate: keeps the top speed tier free of MTG-style unbounded stack/priority-passing complexity (the single largest AI/netcode cost `../interaction-stack.md` already flags), while Quick/Reactive still provide real interaction against everything below Instant. The real Reactive-vs-Instant difference is what happens *after* playing, not *when*: Instant resolves with no response window; Reactive goes through the normal Pending/response process.
→ `../economy.md` (remove the `^` marker section), `../interaction-stack.md` (major rewrite), `../overview.md` §1/§3, `../glossary.md`.

### D44 — Terminology: retire "Unit," adopt Actor/Object, confirm "permanent" spans both
The glossary's "Unit = synonym for Creature" is retired. **Actor** = {Champion, Companion, Creature} — the three types holding their own AP, acting under their own power (already used informally in `../structures-items.md`'s "unlike the three actors"). **Object** = {Structure, Item, Grave, Ruin, Terrain} — everything else, needing funding/direction from something else. **Permanent** = Actor or Object, i.e. everything on the Island — confirmed to match `../ownership.md`'s existing usage, no change there. **"Actor" is tentative** — adopted because it's already load-bearing in the docs, not because it's fully liked; revisit naming later. Note: D47's Activation Capacity for Objects blurs the old "Actor has AP, Object doesn't" boundary somewhat — flagged, not a blocker. → `../glossary.md`, `../ownership.md` (terminology only), `../structures-items.md`.

### D43 — Areas: distance/line/listing hex-set primitive
A formal primitive for an effect's target hex-set: **distance** (all hexes within N), **line** (hexes along a line through specified hexes), **listing** (an explicit hex list). Closed set for now — more shapes added later only as specific cards need them. Unless stated otherwise, an area describes hexes, not occupants. Fills a real gap (AoE targeting was previously unformalized). → `../overview.md` §2.

### D42 — Subterranean bundles Underground access and concealment; adds Ascend/Descend
A Unit needs the **Subterranean** keyword to exist Underground at all — access is gated (pessimistic default, D14), closing a real gap (nothing previously specified how a unit gets Underground/Below). Subterranean and concealment are one package: the keyword buys both Underground access *and* the existing structural hidden-by-default treatment Below already had (D12). Subterranean is the **default** gate, not an absolute wall — a specific card effect can still force a non-Subterranean unit Underground as an explicit exception. New abilities: **Ascend** (`3AP`, Underground → Surface) and **Descend** (`3AP`, Surface → Underground). → `../overview.md` §4, `../asymmetric-information.md`, `../glossary.md`.

### D41 — Levels renamed: Ground/Above/Below → Surface/Air/Underground
Pure relabeling, no mechanical content — same three levels (D12), same capacities. → `../overview.md` §2, `../glossary.md`, all cross-references.

### D40 — Structure/Ruin capacity: two slots per hex, superseding D31; Ruins occupy their slot
**Supersedes D31.** A hex now has **two** Structure/Ruin slots, not one-per-layer: a shared **Surface** slot (Ground+Above combined — Air isn't expected to be structure-dense enough to need its own) and a separate **Underground** slot (concealment kept as its own dedicated axis). Legal cast layer(s) are still card-data-driven (D32, unchanged). Accepted consequence: a Ground and an Above Structure can no longer coexist on one hex either, since they now share a slot. **A destroyed Structure leaves a Ruin in whichever slot it occupied, and — unlike a Grave — a Ruin occupies its slot.** Building a new Structure directly onto a ruined slot replaces the ruin in one action (no separate clear step); a ruin can also be cleared directly by an explicit effect, independent of building. Graves stay completely outside this system: free, unlimited, never competing for a slot. → `../structures-items.md` (rewrite Structure occupancy), `../glossary.md` (Ruin as distinct from Grave).

### D39 — No umbrella term needed for "anything cast" — every card type shares one procedure
The "anything cast through the Aether" umbrella framing (D17) is dropped — no longer needed now that the domain split (D37) makes the card-vs-trace distinction explicit; every card type (Creature, Companion, Spell, Structure, Item) already goes through the identical generic play-a-card procedure. → `../glossary.md`, `../structures-items.md` + `../companions.md` (reword "cast through the Aether like any Spell," which reads circular now — say "cast like any card" instead).

### D38 — Aether restructured: Past / Now / Pending / Future
The Aether gains a fourth region: **Past** (resolved traces, behind Now), **Now** (the point, not a section), **Pending** (the "immediate future" — next to resolve; functionally today's stack), and **Future** (traces already instantiated but scheduled later than "next" — genuinely new: enables delayed effects and a re-cast pushed further out than the very next slot, not redundant with Pending). → `../overview.md` §1, `../interaction-stack.md`, `../glossary.md`.

### D37 — Mind/Aether/Matter domains adopted; Card Zone gains a real Discard, does not reopen D16
Adopts a narrative/organizational frame — **Mind** (Card Zone), **Aether**, **Matter** (Island) — each with its own past/present/future structure: Mind = Discard/Hand/Library; Aether = Past/Now/Pending/Future (D38); Matter = Ruins&Graves/"the rest" (no future slot — physical objects have no ordered future queue). Confirmed **narrative/organizational, not a new mechanical query layer** — no card asks "which domain is X in." Card Zone = {Library, Hand, **Discard**}; Card Zone and the Aether-as-domain-header are mostly documentation scaffolding — "Island" is the term actually used in practice. **A real Discard pile is added.** Does **not** reopen D16: D16 rejected a discard/graveyard specifically to avoid the MTG pattern where a card only leaves play when its permanent dies. This Discard is structurally different — the **card** discharges into Discard the instant it's cast, fully decoupled from the resulting **permanent**'s later lifecycle; a dying creature still only ever produces a Grave (Matter's own separate "past," D16 unchanged). Closes a real implementation gap: current code (`SpellPipeline.cs`, `HandCardRemovedIntent`) deletes a cast card with no destination at all. → `../overview.md` §1 (rewrite), `../glossary.md`, `../ownership.md` (no change — reconfirmed consistent with D16 as refined 2026-08-11).

### D36 — Zone count reaffirmed at four; Hand/Library/Aether-merge considered and rejected; spatial vs. temporal is a doc/implementation grouping, not a zone
Surfaced while restructuring `docs/rules/` around Zones as a top-level concept (post-D16): a hand-drawn timeline visualization (Traces → `now` → Hand → Stack → Library) prompted considering whether Hand, Library, and Aether should collapse into one unified zone (two zones total, alongside Play). **Considered and rejected — D16's four zones stand: Hand, Library, Play, Aether.**

Two initial objections to merging (private-per-player Hand/Library vs. the shared Stack/Trace; a Library hidden even from its own owner) turned out not to be structural blockers — both are just instances of the existing perception-layer default-visibility mechanism (already precedented by Below-layer concealment, D12), not properties that require separate zones. (Library-hidden-from-its-own-owner is a genuinely new *value* on the visibility query — "hidden even from P where P = owner" hadn't shown up before — but not a new mechanism.) The "named regions within one zone" shape proposed for the merge is also a real, valid pattern — Play already works exactly this way (Ground/Above/Below are named regions of one zone, each with its own light internal shape). Unlike Play's hex coordinates, though, nothing today needs a literal shared coordinate space across the temporal regions — Library's order and a trace's fade window are each locally self-contained — so a merge would buy structural tidiness, not new capability.

**Decisive test:** would card text or rules text ever reference the merged zone by its own umbrella name, rather than always naming a specific region ("hand," "library," "stack," "trace")? No — so the merge doesn't earn its keep; it's a rename, not a simplification. **This same test does not extend to un-merging Stack/Trace** (already unified as Aether, D16): that merger was never justified by wanting a shared name — it's justified by object identity persisting when a resolved trace is pushed back across `now` to re-cast it (flashback-style). Aether stays one zone with two named positions, exactly as Play stays one zone with three named layers.

**Adopted instead of a zone merger:** a **documentation/implementation-level grouping** — a "spatial" view (Play) and a "temporal" view (Hand, Library, Aether) — for organizing the rules docs and engine state, without claiming there are fewer zones. → `../glossary.md`, `../overview.md` §1, `PLAN.md` §9.

### D35 — Triggered abilities are a fundamental ability kind, universal and independently costable
Triggered abilities ("when X happens, do Y", `../interaction-stack.md`) are not a Structure-specific pattern — they sit alongside **activated** abilities as one of the two fundamental ability shapes, available to any permanent or actor regardless of Type: a Creature, Structure, Champion, or Companion can carry one directly, and an Item can grant one to its carrier via equip (same funding rules as any granted ability, D26). The two shapes differ in when a cost, if any, is paid: an **activated** ability's cost is paid upfront, by choice, to put it on the stack at all; a **triggered** ability goes on the stack automatically the instant its condition is met, with no activation choice — but it can still carry its own cost, paid **at resolution**, typically as an optional "you may pay" clause (e.g. "whenever a creature enters within distance 2, you may pay 2 mana; if you do, draw a card") rather than an upfront requirement. Funding for that resolution-time cost follows the ordinary ownership-tree payment walk (D26) exactly like any other cost; only the controller (D28) decides whether to pay. → `../interaction-stack.md`, `../structures-items.md`.

### D34 — Item has no Life; loose Items are exempt from layer capacity
Unlike Structure (D24, attackable, has Life), an **Item never has Life** — it's never a combat participant (no Attack, no AP, never a legal attack/ranged target the way a Structure or creature is), so a Life total would only matter for hypothetical generic-damage/AoE destruction, which nothing requires. An Item is removed from play only via an explicit printed effect (e.g. "destroy target Item", a sacrifice cost) — never by combat damage. Separately: a **loose Item does not consume any of a layer's 3-creature capacity** (D12) — layer capacity is a creature-only cap; any number of loose Items may share a (terrain, layer) address (exact board visualization TBD, not a rules concern). → `../structures-items.md`.

### D33 — Casting/materialization via the Aether: target locked before resolution; illegal target fizzles per-instruction, not a whole-spell counter
Casting a Creature, Structure, or Item follows one procedure: choose the spell → choose target terrain + layer → pay cost → the spell goes into the **Aether** (a trace ahead of the stack's `now` marker, `../interaction-stack.md`, D16) → when `now` reaches it, it resolves: the Aether trace stays put and its resolution creates a separate, linked permanent at the chosen (terrain, layer) — not the trace itself relocating (D16, refined 2026-08-11). Because the board can change while a spell sits in the Aether (opponent responses, contested terrain, a layer filling up), resolution re-checks legality. **If a chosen target is no longer legal, that specific instruction fizzles — the spell does not resolve that part — rather than the whole spell being countered MTG-style.** For a single-target permanent cast (today's only case) this looks identical to a counter in outcome, but the principle is general: a future multi-effect spell with several targets only loses the parts that went illegal, not the whole effect. Paid cost is never refunded either way. This is a general Aether-resolution rule (relates to D16), not specific to Structures/Items. → `../structures-items.md`, `../interaction-stack.md`.

### D32 — Location model unified across Creature/Structure/Item; layer is card-derived, not a free choice
A permanent's board location is uniformly **(terrain cell, layer)** — Creature (already true, D12), Structure (D31), and now a **loose** Item (closes the open "where does a loose item live" question from D25): a dropped item lands exactly where it was dropped, layer included (an underground creature's dropped sword ends up at (that cell, Below), hidden by the same rule that already hides Below creatures). An **equipped** Item's location is instead its carrying actor (D26) — not spatial at all, since it travels with that actor. **Legal layer(s) at cast time come from the card's own data/keywords, never a free choice by default**: in the overwhelming majority of cases exactly one layer is legal (no keyword → Ground; Flying → Above; a burrower-type keyword → Below), matching the pessimistic-default posture (D14) — a card may explicitly print a choice among multiple legal layers as opt-in flexibility, but there's no generic "pick any layer" capability. Applies identically to Creature and Structure casts. → `../structures-items.md`.

### D31 — Structure gains a Layer; capacity becomes 1-per-layer, not 1-per-hex
Amends D24. A Structure's location is no longer just "the terrain cell" — it now includes a **Layer** (Ground/Above/Below, D12), and its dedicated slot's capacity is **1 per layer**, not 1 per hex: a single terrain cell can hold up to three Structures simultaneously, one per layer (e.g. a surface keep, a sky platform, and a sunken vault, all on the same cell). Mechanism is unchanged — still a dedicated slot, capacity 1, independent of creature layer capacity (D12's 3-per-layer creature cap) — just applied per layer instead of once per hex. Bonus: a Structure occupying Below is hidden by default, for free, via the same rule that already hides Below creatures (D12/pillar 6) — solves "a structure hidden inside a building/underwater" without inventing a containment mechanism. → `../structures-items.md`.

### D30 — Un-equip joins the default-action set, free and uncapped; Zone of Control rejected as a default
**Un-equip** is a new default ability every eligible actor (Champion, Companion, Creature) carries alongside Move/Attack/Defend/Equip — `0AP`, completely free, no once-per-turn cap (unlike Defend, D15, there's no known balance reason to restrict it). Drops a carried Item back to loose on the actor's current hex, removing the actor as its parent (D26). Closes the open item flagged in the Structures/Items design pass. **Separately, Zone of Control** (does a creature block/threaten enemy movement through its adjacent hexes) — surfaced in the same discussion, considered, and **rejected as a default**: handing every creature a passage-blocking capability for free is exactly the generous-default pattern D14 warns against (already flagged as a candidate in `history/pessimistic-default-audit.md` before this session). If added at all, it's a **positive keyword** on specific creatures, never a base rule. → `../ownership.md`, `../structures-items.md`, `history/pessimistic-default-audit.md`.

### D29 — Companion-death controller loss is derived, not a choice; real open question is uncontrolled-creature behavior
Corrects D27's framing. Whether a creature loses its **controller** when the Companion that summoned it dies was never actually an open design choice — it falls directly out of D28's definition: if climbing the parent chain doesn't reach a live Champion, there is no controller, full stop, no special case needed. **The genuinely open question is what an uncontrolled-but-still-living creature actually does.** Terrain and Structure are inert, so "uncontrolled" just means nobody may activate them — but a creature still holds and refills its own AP (D10, always self-paid, never climbed regardless of controller), so it isn't obviously inert the same way. Not designed here: whether it can still act at all, and if so, who/what directs it. **This opens a larger, deliberately-parked design space:** mission/PvE content with NPC-like creatures running an AI-driven routine, independent of either player — an uncontrolled creature (e.g. one orphaned by a Companion's death) as one possible source of such an NPC, with killing one as a potential bonus objective. Parked in `PLAN.md` §9, not designed now. → `../ownership.md`.

### D28 — Terminology correction: "controller" is a strict, Champion-only walk; "payment" is a separate, shorter one
D26/D27 used "controller" loosely for two different things: (a) whoever actually pays a cost (which can be a Companion), and (b) whose side a permanent is on. These are genuinely different walks over the same parent chain and need different names. **Payment:** climb from the entity itself and stop at the **first** node holding the needed resource — AP/Life stop immediately (self, D10); mana stops at a Companion if one is found before reaching the Champion, since a Companion is the one non-Champion node able to hold a resource at all. **Controller:** climb **all the way to the top**, regardless of what's held along the way. **A Companion is never itself a root** — it always has a live Champion parent for as long as it's in play — so this walk, if it resolves, always resolves to a Champion. **"If the root is a Champion, that Champion is the permanent's controller"** — stated conditionally, deliberately not asserting the climb always succeeds (that's what D27's open ToDo is about: whether it still resolves through a dead intermediate). Consequence worth naming: a permanent's payer and controller can differ — anything rooted through a Companion is *paid for* by that Companion but *controlled* by the Champion who cast it. A card costing "this creature's controller pays 2 life" therefore always hits the Champion's Life, even for a Companion-summoned creature — intentional, not an edge case. Also corrects D24's/`../resources-terrain.md`'s looser "terrain/structure is controlled by whoever bonds it (Champion or Companion)" language: that's the **payment** relationship, not the strict controller — a Companion-bonded terrain/structure is *funded* by that Companion but *controlled* by that Companion's own Champion. → `../ownership.md`.

### D27 — Ownership tree refined: no creature-parents-creature; terrain gets three bond states; inner-node removal uncontrols successors
Amends D26 after further discussion. **Creature is always a leaf for creation purposes:** when a creature's own ability produces another creature or a structure, that new permanent's parent resolves **directly, once, at creation** to whichever Champion/Companion recursively pays for it — never to the creating creature. A creature remains a valid parent for **Items** it carries (a distinct "carrying" relationship, not creation/funding), so a creature's death still orphans its equipped items (**confirmed**: they become ownerless, reverting to loose). **Terrain now has three states, not two:** bonded-**active**; bonded-**interrupted** (D8's existing enemy-on-path "pause," now formally named — behaves as "no parent" for every downstream purpose including Structure control, reverts to active automatically once the path clears, no re-Bond needed); and **unbonded** (the link is actually gone, needs a fresh Bond). **New ways to reach unbonded**, beyond the existing Companion-death cascade (D22): the Champion's own voluntary choice to drop one specific bond (a granular sibling to the existing all-at-once Collapse Network, D9); "unbond from target land" as an additional cost printed on a card (new cost primitive); and a hostile targeted effect ("target Champion/Companion unbonds from target land") as a new attack-the-economy effect archetype, distinct from D8's positional pause. **General rule:** removing (or interrupting) an inner node uncontrols all its successors. Confirmed for Terrain→Structure (deliberate — opens area-control fights over contested terrain, interrupting or severing a bond either way cuts a Structure loose). **Open ToDo, explicitly not decided:** whether the same applies to Companion→Creature — i.e. does a creature lose its *controller* (not just its funding root) when the Companion that summoned it dies, or does control survive via a static link even though funding breaks. → `../ownership.md`.

### D26 — General ownership tree: every permanent has one parent; costs climb to a controller
**Supersedes the funding specifics of D24/D25** with one general mechanism. Every entity other than a Champion has exactly **one parent** — Companion → the Champion that cast it; Creature → whoever/whatever summoned it (Champion, Companion, **or another creature**, transitively); Terrain → whoever currently bonds it (dynamic, unchanged from D8/D22); Structure → the terrain it occupies (one hop removed, inherits Terrain's dynamism for free); Item while equipped → its equipping actor; Item while unequipped → **no parent, and no usable ability at all** (see below). Walking parents upward always terminates at a **controller** (a Champion or Companion). **Paying a cost:** AP and Life are held directly by every actor/creature (D10) and are always **self-paid**, never climbed — this is why AP needed no bespoke exception in the original framing, and why Life resolves the same way (a creature's "pay 2 life" spends its own Life, not its controller's, unless a card explicitly names the controller as payer). Every other cost type (mana, discard a card, sacrifice a permanent) **climbs the parent chain live** until it reaches a controller, who pays from its own pool (shared for the Champion, private for a Companion). **Live walk, not a cached snapshot:** a break anywhere in the chain (not just the topmost root) orphans everything below it from non-AP/non-Life costs. **Only Champions cast spells** (no Hand on a Companion) — this alone explains why a discard cost printed on a Companion's ability would climb past it to the Champion, no special case needed. **Amends D22:** "a Companion can't share mana" is narrowed, not repealed — a Companion funds its *entire subtree* (creatures/terrain/structures/items rooted to it) from its private pool; it still can't fund a *different* controller's own abilities directly. **Item's funding resolved via a generic ability, not a DAG:** every eligible actor gets `2AP: Equip target Item sharing this location` (tuning baseline); combined with unequipped items having zero usable abilities, this keeps the whole model a tree (an earlier multi-parent/DAG draft for loose items was considered and rejected as unnecessary complexity) — equip is removable/re-priceable per creature (pillar 5), stealing is a costed action via the same ability, and exclusivity (one holder at a time) falls out for free. **Open:** what happens to an equipped Item when its carrier dies (presumed: reverts to loose, unconfirmed); whether chain-breaking should be a live walk or a cached root captured at creation time (flagged as an extension beyond what was explicitly stated). → `../ownership.md`, amends `../companions.md`, `../structures-items.md`.

### D25 — Item: equip is an explicit AP action; funding follows the carrier
Resolves the "Item" open in D17/`../economy.md`. **Equip is an explicit AP-costed action**, not automatic-on-move. **Superseded/refined by D26:** the equip mechanism turned out to be a **generic ability** every eligible actor holds (`2AP: Equip target Item sharing this location`, tuning baseline), and an equipped Item's funding is just the ownership-tree default (D26) applied to its parent — the equipping actor's own pool. This also resolved exclusivity (one parent = one holder) and avoided a multi-parent/DAG model for loose items. Still open: where a loose Item sits spatially, un-equip/drop mechanics, and what happens to an equipped Item when its carrier dies. → `../structures-items.md`, `../ownership.md`.

### D24 — Structure: dedicated per-hex slot; control mirrors Terrain's current bonder, not the builder
Resolves the "Structure" opens in D17/`../economy.md`. **Occupancy:** a Structure gets its own dedicated **per-terrain-cell slot, capacity 1** — separate from the Ground/Above/Below layers (D12), so it never competes with creatures for room (visually centered on the hex). **Cast like a Creature** (D20 pattern): onto a bonded cell in the caster's realm with a free Structure slot, paid in shared-pool mana. **Attackable, has Life**, 0 Life → grave/ruins in Play (D14/D16 pattern); "indestructible" is a keyword, not default. **No default effect on movement/LOS** — a specific card (e.g. a Building subtype) may print that it blocks either, as cell-property data. **Control is dynamic, not fixed at build time:** a Structure's controller is whoever **currently bonds** the terrain cell it sits on — the exact same rule as Terrain control (D8/D22), not a separate ownership state. Deliberate consequence: contested/newly-unbonded terrain can be re-bonded by the opposing root, **capturing the Structure positionally** without attacking it — fits D8's "denial is positional and reversible" shape. **Funding:** the controlling root's own pool (shared/private per D22), same split as Terrain abilities. **Default cost is mana-only** (mirrors Terrain — a Structure has no AP of its own, activated "like casting a spell"); a specific printed ability **may** additionally cost the controller's own AP as card-level data (pillar 5) — this same flexibility now also applies to Terrain's own abilities (amends the D8 terrain-abilities note, which read as strictly mana-only). **Generalized by D26:** "control follows the current bonder" turned out to be one instance of a general ownership-tree rule (a Structure's parent is the terrain it occupies; the terrain's own parent is whoever bonds it) rather than a Structure-specific mechanism. → `../structures-items.md`, `../resources-terrain.md`, `../ownership.md`.

### D23 — Instant-speed marker (`^`) for activated abilities: default sorcery-speed
Generalizes D5's card-level instant/sorcery-speed split down to the **ability** level, and removes the need for a Champion-specific "can abilities be reactive?" special case (supersedes the former Open question 3 in `../champions.md`). **Default: sorcery-speed** — any AP-costed ability (creature, Champion, or Companion alike) is playable only during its controller's own Action phase, and only while the stack (the Aether's pending region, D16) is empty — not as a response. **`^` marks an ability instant-speed:** usable in any priority window (D5), on either player's turn, same as a card-level instant. **Composes freely with the existing AP-cost flavors** — `^2*AP`, `^3!AP`, `^1AP` are all valid: `^` governs *when*, `!`/`*` govern *how the AP is consumed*, independent axes. **No new resource:** an instant-speed ability still spends the actor's own AP from its normal (refills-on-your-turn-only) budget, so acting reactively means that AP had to be held in reserve since the actor's own last turn — reactive plays cost you offense earlier, which is presumably the intended tension, not a gap (flag for playtest). → `../economy.md`, `../champions.md`.

### D22 — Companion: a new card type between Creature and Champion; second network root
**New card type**, deckbuilding-gated to specific Champion(s). Makes the game's channeling hierarchy explicit: **Creature** spends mana but can't draw it; **Companion** draws mana (bonds terrain) but can't share it — it keeps a **private mana pool**, spendable only on its own printed abilities; **Champion** draws *and* shares (D8/D9 unchanged). Shape: Creature's three numbers (Attack/Life/AP, D10) **plus** a private mana pool **plus** an AP-costed **Bond** ability (`x*AP`, same cost flavor as the Champion's `2*AP` Bond, D9 — priced more restrictively against the Companion's smaller AP total, so bonding crowds out most of its turn). Cast like a Creature (D20: drawn, paid in mana, summoned onto a bonded terrain). **Extends D8 rather than replacing it:** a Companion in play is a **second network root**, bonding terrain reachable from itself via already-bonded terrain, same reachability/pause mechanics as the Champion's root. **Ownership follows the bonder** — whichever root (Champion or a specific Companion) performs a bond determines which pool that terrain feeds (shared vs. that Companion's private pool); **a Companion's death un-bonds its own terrain outright** (reverts to unbonded, re-bondable later by any surviving root — same "undo, don't pause" vocabulary as un-summon, D16), since a Companion's bond is only as permanent as the Companion itself, unlike the Champion's (D8). Mana never crosses the shared/private boundary in either direction — this also governs **terrain control**: a terrain is controlled by whichever root bonded it (or by no one, if unbonded), and any of that terrain's own printed activated abilities can only be funded from the pool that node feeds (shared for Champion-bonded, that Companion's private pool for Companion-bonded). See `../resources-terrain.md`. Move/Attack are also **network-dependent costs**, mirroring the Champion's realm-constraint shape (D9): cheaper once the Companion's own connection collapses, pricier while it stays connected. **Invariant preserved:** still exactly two resources, mana + AP (economy.md) — a Companion adds a second *pool instance* of mana, not a third resource. **Open:** deck/board count limits, whether Companions gate faction identity, meta-progression shape (parked to the meta-layer, post-M4); also surfaced a pre-existing table/prose contradiction in D9's Champion Move-cost description that needs a fix. → `../companions.md`.

### D21 — Turn structure: Beginning / Action / End; per-action response windows
**Beginning of turn:** mana/AP **refresh** (covers creatures and the Champion alike, D9) — **simultaneously, no state checks**; then the active player puts their beginning-of-turn triggers into the Aether, then the opponent theirs (**APNAP** order → opponent's resolve first, LIFO). **No priority in this phase to start** (simple); active-player *choices* (upkeep costs) still work — only *opponent-reactive* upkeep needs a window, added later as a **data change** (phases are data, D3). *(Card draw is no longer an automatic Beginning-phase step — as of the D9 revision it's the Champion's `5*AP: Draw` action, taken in the Action phase.)* **Action phase:** active player plays spells, spends AP to move/attack/draw/bond/activate, in any order, until they choose to finish; **after every active-player action — including a move and including a deliberate pass — the inactive player gets a response window** (refines D5: windows now open per-action, not only on stack/Combat events; enables movement-triggered traps; kept fast by auto-pass + sorcery-speed defaults). **End of turn:** end-of-turn triggers, APNAP, same as beginning. **Refresh (not bank) resolves D8 open #4.** Remaining tuning: starting hand size, hand cap, mulligan. Deck **legality = format** concern (1v1), not core. → `../overview.md` §3, PLAN §2.

### D20 — Summoning: onto a bonded terrain in your realm; mana cost on card
A creature is summoned onto a **bonded terrain cell within the player's realm/mana network** (so the **realm is the deployment zone as well as the economy** — the summoning frontier creeps with the realm). Cost = the card's **mana** (a plain mana-economy play, unrelated to the Champion's own AP, D9), payable multiple times/turn as mana allows. Constraints: a **free slot on the creature's layer** (D4/D12). **Open (rec pessimistic, D14):** **summoning sickness** — a summoned creature **cannot act the turn it enters** by default; "acts immediately" is a positive (haste-like) keyword. → `../overview.md` §4.

### D19 — Cross-layer combat: initiation is layer-restricted, retaliation is universal
Default attack **initiation** legality (attacker → target): **Ground→**Ground ✓, Flyer ✗, Sub only-if-located. **Flyer→**Ground ✓, Flyer ✓, Sub only-if-located. **Sub→**Ground ✓ *(tentative, balance)*, Flyer ✗, Sub ✓ (if located). Subterrain is **hidden by default** (D12) → cannot be targeted unless located. All defaults; cards give exceptions (reach/anti-air/true-sight). **Retaliation is UNIVERSAL (corrects an earlier "gated by matrix" reading):** a defender **always** deals its damage back to the attacker regardless of layer — a ground creature retaliates against a diving flyer even though it can't *initiate* on flyers. Combat is mutual across layers; air superiority = an **initiative / target-selection** advantage, not damage-immunity. **Sole exception: Ranged** — a keyword making the attack **one-way** (no retaliation): "deals damage equal to its power to target creature." (Ranged combat otherwise untackled; covered by keyword later.) **Acting reveals a concealed sub:** attacking **surfaces** it → it stays **located while it remains on that hex**, and **re-conceals only when it moves away** *(interpreted from user's note — confirm next session)*. "Strike and stay hidden" is a rare positive keyword. This *is* D18 (a concealed thing risks revealing via the hard fact of acting). → `../overview.md` §4.

### D18 — Belief-consistency model for asymmetric information (the hard core of pillar 6)
**Principle:** *a soft claim is cheap and holds until a hard fact tests it, then the truth is applied and the lie collapses.* The engine **never fabricates a consistent lie** (rejected: regress/unbalanceable/unauthorable); **cards author claims**, the engine projects them. **No deduction engine** — the engine guarantees hard facts are truthful and enforces a **small, finite set of conservation laws** (e.g. *claimed mana costs this turn ≤ public network max*, card-count conservation) that auto-collapse mechanically-impossible claims; **all other deduction is the human's** (UI surfaces hard facts). **Collapse → full truth** by default (incl. which of a masked pool it was).
**Resource border (a principle, not a list):** *standing state public; the private-resource ledger hidden; any cost with a board footprint self-revealing* ⇒ **a spell is mimickable iff its whole cost is payable from the hidden mana ledger.** Defaults: mana **network public** (needed for denial, D8), mana **spend/live-balance hidden** (engine of cost-deception + "mana for a trick?" bluffs), **hand size public** (count; contents private), **AP public** (max & current — surprises come from instant-speed *timing*, D5, not hidden state), footprint costs (sacrifice/unbond/discard) **self-revealing**. Only standing quantity hidden = **live mana balance** (minimal carve-out from D7). Interacts with D8 #4 (bank vs refresh).
**Representation:** the atomic primitive is a **`face`** — a per-observer overlay (claimed identity + stat modifiers) a **permanent** carries; this **unifies** creature-mimicry (enter *with* a face) and spell-mimicry (an effect *applies* a face), with the card's trace showing the same face. Composable combinators — **`ChooseOne[…]`** (modal, private caster choice → true state) and **`Mimic(face)`** (applies a face over an object's whole Aether life — stack + trace, hiding true identity). Key capability: **events/effects carry a per-observer projected form, not just objects** — and since `face` is itself an effect, projecting it **mutates the opponent's view state, board objects included** (a buffed creature carries a *true* modifier + a per-observer *claimed* modifier: **modifiers can be per-observer**). Trace and board stay consistent (one authored `face`); the divergence rides along until a hard fact collapses it. Honest and bluff casts of the same Mimic card are **indistinguishable** to the opponent. **Open:** default spell-resolution visibility (lean Model 2: true by default, Mimic overrides). → `../asymmetric-information.md`, `../overview.md` §9.

### D17 — Card-type taxonomy: type is a queryable tag
**Card type is a data-driven queryable tag (pillar 5), not a fixed class** — the engine has a type system with default behaviors per tag; cards can bend type; the type *list* is content that grows. Structural backbone = *what resolution leaves behind*:
- **Play-permanents:** **Creature** (mobile; Attack/Life/AP, D10), **Structure** (stationary, **non-carryable**, location-based; **subtypes** e.g. *Building* + others — absorbs non-equippable "artifacts"), **Item** (**carryable** — pick up / equip). Dead permanents → **graves/ruins in Play** (D16).
- **Trace-only:** **Spell** (the one-shot type; resolves to an Aether trace, no permanent).
**Outside the card-type list** (pre-game / separate economy, never cast through the Aether): **Map, Champion, Terrain**. **Type-as-tag with subtypes** is adopted (not a fixed enum). ("Artifact" dissolves: equippable → Item, stationary → Structure.)

Still open there: loose-Item board footprint, un-equip mechanics, carry exclusivity. → PLAN §3.2; card-taxonomy step in `history/knowledge-capture-plan.md`.

### D16 — Unified zone model: Hand / Library / Play / Aether; no graveyard; objects are single-zone, linked across zones (refined 2026-08-11)
Zones: **Hand, Library** (deck), **Play** (permanents on the Board), and **Aether** — a per-observer **timeline**. The Aether unifies the **stack** and the spell-**graveyard**: a spell is one object that slides `Hand → front of the `now` marker (pending, LIFO = the stack) → across `now` → a **trace** in the past`. So stack and graveyard are the **same zone at different positions relative to `now`**, not two zones (one object moving, never copied). Abilities also leave traces. **No graveyard zone:** dead **creatures become graves in Play**, keeping a board location (spatial reanimation, pillar 2); spells live as Aether traces. Aether knowledge is **per-observer** (pillar 6) — your view of history is itself hideable. ~~The engine also allows a card in multiple zones at once (digital-only) — kept as a capability, not a default/mechanic until a concrete use earns it.~~ *(superseded below.)*
**Resolved:** (1) **Traces fade** on a **configurable** window — this is what makes the model a net *simplification* (recent past interactable, old past drops away) and a color-specialization axis. (2) **Graves take no slot** — hex-level markers (view-toggleable UI). (3) **Timeline distance is mechanical** — a spell can push a trace **into the future** to **re-cast** it; such time-manipulation cards are **deferred** (start simple) but the axis is real, keep it open. (4) ~~Multi-zone, concretely: casting a Play-permanent leaves both a permanent in Play and an anchored summon/build trace in the Aether.~~ *(mechanism superseded below; the kill-vs-un-summon outcome it describes is unchanged.)* (5) **Terminology confirmed:** zones are **Hand / Library / Play / Aether**; **Board** stays the spatial grid; **Discard/Graveyard deleted**; card text says *traces*/*graves*, never "graveyard." → glossary, `../overview.md` §1, `../interaction-stack.md`.

**Refined 2026-08-11 — object identity across zones, supersedes "multi-zone" above (kept struck-through for history).** Surfaced while restructuring `docs/rules/` around Zones as a top-level concept: "a card in multiple zones at once" was the wrong mechanism for an outcome that's still correct. Corrected model: **every object lives in exactly one zone at a time.** An object in one zone can *create* new objects in other zones, linked to it — but it is never itself present in two zones, and identity does not survive a zone crossing (an object doesn't relocate between zones; the source object is consumed and a new, linked object is created in the destination zone). Concretely: casting a Creature/Structure/Item consumes the Hand object and creates a new Aether trace object; when that trace resolves (crosses `now`), it does **not** relocate into Play — it stays in the Aether as a past trace, and its resolution *spawns* a separate, linked Play-permanent object. This reproduces D16's original kill-vs-un-summon design exactly, just as two objects instead of one: **kill** targets the Play object directly (Life → 0 → grave — an in-zone state change, not a zone crossing, so the same object stays put); **un-summon** deletes the linked Aether trace, which cascades to delete the linked Play object with no death trigger, only while the trace hasn't faded. A token-creating effect (e.g. a Companion's built-in summon ability resolving) creates a Play object directly with no card-lineage at all — zero-origin creation, not an exception to the model. → `../glossary.md`, `../overview.md` §1, `../structures-items.md` D33.

### D15 — Defend rule: RESOLVED to `0*AP` — free, but once per turn
**Resolved 2026-08-09**, superseding the two-variant playtest below (kept for history). Defending costs **`0*AP`**: free (no AP check at all, in either direction), but gated to **at most once per turn per actor** via the same `*` once-per-turn flavor Bond/Draw already use (`Query.CanUseOncePerTurnAction`, keyed on `CoreAbilities.Defend`) — collapses the redundant `defended`-flag + AP into the existing once-per-turn mechanism instead of a bespoke flag.

**Why not V1 (Exhaust) — a concrete bug, not just a feel-bad:** V1 gated defending on remaining AP. Since default Attack already exhausts (`3!AP`, drains the attacker to 0), the very first combat between two mirror creatures (both attack + defend once, mutual damage) leaves *both* sides at 0 AP. But AP only refreshes on its own controller's Beginning phase — the defender (whose controller's turn is next) refreshes almost immediately, while the attacker (whose controller's turn is a full round away) stays at 0 AP through the *entire* following opponent turn. In that window the former defender can attack the still-exhausted former attacker for a free, undefended kill — so attacking first was strictly worse than being attacked, an incentive *against* attacking that directly fights the intended combat-forward design. `0*AP` removes this because Defend never reads AP: an actor that spent all its AP attacking (or on anything else) can still defend later.

**Why not flat free-and-unlimited (V2) either:** V2 also fixes the bug (no AP check), but drops the per-turn cap entirely — a single creature could defend against every attack that lands on its hex in one turn, with only Life as a brake. `0*AP` keeps V1's "defend once, then done" flavor (the once-per-turn gate), just decoupled from the thing that was actually broken (the AP check), rather than throwing away the cap along with the bug.

**Escape valve for the "tap cost" flavor cards may still want:** decoupling Defend from AP means a strong ability can no longer *incidentally* deny a creature's block by draining its AP (the way MTG's tap-to-activate denies blocking). That's now an explicit, card-level, opt-in effect instead of a base rule: a printed ability's own effect can additionally consume the actor's Defend for the turn (emit the same `OncePerTurnActionUsedIntent(actor, CoreAbilities.Defend)` Defend itself uses) — e.g. "5!AP, 3 mana: draw a card **and this creature can't defend this turn**." Pure card data, no new engine mechanism, and — critically — it never applies to the base Attack ability, so the original punish-the-attacker bug can't come back through the front door.

**Cross-reference, updated 2026-08-09:** the Champion-retaliation question in `../champions.md` (Open questions, "Champion retaliation") is effectively answered by this: the Champion defends through the exact same `Query.CanDefend`/`ResolveDefendCost` as any creature, no bespoke Option A/B needed. Note this **reverses** that question's originally-stated "desired shape" ("a Champion that spent its whole turn's resources elsewhere should no longer be able to retaliate") — under `0*AP` it *can* retaliate regardless of what else it spent AP on, once per turn, unless a specific card explicitly consumes its Defend as described above. → `../champions.md`.

**Superseded playtest packages (kept for history):**
- **V1 — Exhaust (`1!AP`):** defending costs `1!AP`. Rejected — see the bug above.
- **V2 — Delete defend-once:** defending free and unlimited, no per-turn cap; Life the only limiter. Rejected in favor of `0*AP`, which keeps V2's fix but restores V1's per-turn cap.

**Alternative not adopted, kept for a future playtest:** a "recovery pulse" idea — Defend stays AP-gated (back to `1!AP`), paired with a new general rule (every actor regains `1AP` at the *opponent's* Beginning phase too, not just its own) and a new `x!!AP` cost flavor ("double-exhaust" — skips that pulse). Fixes the same V1 bug from a different angle (closes the refresh-timing gap instead of decoupling Defend from AP) and avoids ever special-casing Defend, but touches the core AP-refresh rule and adds a third cost-flavor axis. Full writeup: `history/playtest-variants.md`.
→ `../overview.md` §4, `../economy.md`.

### D14 — Design principle: pessimistic (additive) defaults
**A rule's default is the weakest *still-functional-and-fun* case; cards improve on it via *positive* lines.** Keywords read as granted abilities, not restrictions — negative lines ("may only defend once," "cannot X") are dispreferred as defaults (they feel worse), though allowed occasionally for flavor/balance. **Guardrail:** "worst case" = weakest-but-complete, never literally crippled — the base game must stand on its own as a satisfying game. Companion to "simplest rule that still exposes a hook." **Corollaries decided now:** (a) **Damage persists** across turns; **no automatic end-of-turn healing** — healing is a special ability/effect (fixes the MtG behavior the user dislikes); Life is persistent state, 0 Life → destroyed. (b) **Defend-once is the pessimistic default**; *multi-defend* is a **positive keyword** (Bulwark/Guardian-type), while `cannot defend` remains an occasional negative keyword (D10). → `../overview.md` header + §4; memory `pessimistic-defaults`.

### D13 — Combat damage: mutual, simultaneous, attacker-assigns, gang-up
Accepted package. Scope: sequential → **one attacker vs a hex's declared defenders** now; many-to-many rides the deferred phased scheduler (D3). **Invariant:** a Combat resolves into **directed damage events** (`attacker → target(s)`, `target → attacker`), each independently preventable/modifiable/redirectable (pillar 5) — so Ranged/First Strike/Trample/retaliation-immunity are modifiers, never new rules. **Defaults:** (1) **mutual** — attacker and defenders damage each other (Ranged = "takes no retaliation" keyword); (2) **simultaneous** — both sides' damage computed from the pre-combat state, so "both die" is possible (First Strike = opt-in sequencing keyword); (3) **attacker assigns** its Attack across the declared defenders (engine exposes a damage-assignment decision point); (4) **gang-up** — every declared defender deals its Attack to the attacker. **Undefended** (zero defenders): attacker assigns its Attack among targets of its choice on the hex (creatures, or the Champion → win path), no retaliation. Deferred as keywords/tuning: Ranged/First Strike/Trample/numbers, and **cross-layer targeting** (D12) = the next combat sub-question. → `../overview.md` §4, PLAN §3.4.

### D12 — A hex has three vertical layers (ground / air / sub), each capacity 3
Completing D4's "reserved corners." A hex holds **three occupancy layers** — **ground**, **above (flying)**, **below (submerged/subterranean)** — each with capacity **3** (the tactical-vs-crowding compromise: enough to guard key creatures, not a scrum). The engine represents a cell as three distinct occupancy spaces; adjacency/LOS/combat are potentially per-layer (details deferred). **The below layer is hidden by default** (pillar 6): submerged occupancy appears only in its owner's view; the opponent sees nothing there unless Detection/reveal applies. **Reveal / view-merge is an open perception question** (how a "reveal" spell merges the hidden sub-view into the opponent's view — scope, duration, granularity). Fits the existing per-observer perception layer cleanly (reveal = a visibility-modifier), so it is *structurally* supported already; the mechanic details are content. → `../overview.md` §2, `../asymmetric-information.md`.

### D11 — The map is a fourth match component: a card, sized to the terrain deck
A legal match loadout is **four** things: card deck, **terrain deck**, Champion, **and a map** (all legality-constrained). **Maps are a strategic choice and come in sizes; map size determines terrain-deck size** (map legality is tied to size). A **map card** specifies: (1) **form** — which hex cells exist and their connections; "holes" are **void/aether** terrain (present as data with special functionality, not absent cells); (2) both players' **starting positions**; (3) each player's **home zone** around their start, sized to **exactly the terrain-deck size**, filled **randomly** with that player's terrain deck at game start (this *is* the D8 "home base"); (4) a **neutral area** with a predefined terrain-generation procedure (usually fixed basics, possibly randomized); (5) optional **landmarks** with special rules (e.g., first creature to enter → its Champion draws). The engine consumes **one resolved map** as data (consistent with cell-as-data). **Open (pre-game, deferrable):** in 1v1 both players bring a map — the selection/ban mechanism to pick the one played. → `../overview.md` §2, `../resources-terrain.md`; resolves D8's home-base + terrain↔hex + terrain-deck-size questions.

### D10 — Creatures run on Action Points; mana is one shared pool
**Mana is a single shared pool per player** (channeled by the Champion, D8/D9) drawn on by the Champion's spells/abilities **and every creature's mana-abilities** — this is what makes the Champion a "channeler." **Creatures have a second, private economy: Action Points (AP).** A creature spends its own AP to **move, fight, and activate abilities**; abilities may cost a mix of `mana + AP` (Champion abilities cost the same shape, `mana + AP` — see D9). **A creature is three numbers: Attack / Life / Action Points.** AP **subsumes** the old Movement stat (moving costs AP, rec 1/hex) and the "move-once/attack-once" rules (both are AP purchases → multi-attack/hit-and-run are emergent); **Range demotes to a keyword.** The repeating pattern across the game: *every actor = shared mana + a private AP budget.* **Champion↔AP (confirmed, D9 revised 2026-08-06):** the Champion has its own AP budget and spends it directly, exactly like a creature — one combat/action model for all entities, no gating resource. **Move/attack are default *abilities*, not rules:** every creature has replaceable `1AP: Move` and `3!AP: Attack`. **The `!` cost:** `xAP` spends exactly x; **`x!AP`** requires x then consumes all remaining AP — so `3!` attack = no multi-attack emergently (a non-`!` attack would allow it). **Defending is free**; `cannot defend` is a common keyword, not a default (avoids disincentivizing attacks / board clog). Remaining tuning levers: AP refresh/carryover, Champion base AP. → `../economy.md`; rewrites `../overview.md` §4.

### D9 — Champion runs on mana + Action Points, like a creature; killing it wins
**Revised 2026-08-06** — supersedes the original one-per-turn "Channel" model (kept below for history). The Champion is **not a bespoke third resource model**; it runs the **same two-resource shape as a creature** (D10): **mana** (spent on spells/units, many per turn, shared pool) and its own **Action Points** (private, refills each turn), spent on **draw a card, bond a terrain, move, fight, and activate abilities**. Mechanically the Champion is a special king-like creature — exactly one per side, its death ends the game — not a second combat model (pillar 5). Baseline example costs (tuning, not locked; full ability list still undesigned): `5*AP` draw (replaces the automatic per-turn draw), `2*AP` bond (the D8 once/turn bond limit, now expressed via the new `*` once-per-turn cost flavor — see `../economy.md`), `2AP`/`1AP` move (network-active/collapsed, revised below), `5!AP`/`3!AP` attack (network-active/collapsed — drain-all like a Creature's default attack), `2–5AP` abilities. **Tension** is an open resource-allocation puzzle (`move+fight`, `draw+bond`, `bond+move×2`, …) rather than a hardcoded ternary choice; the classic ramp-vs-spend tension still falls out of the numbers (`Draw+Bond` = the 7 AP baseline). **Win condition unchanged:** killing the enemy Champion wins (Duelyst-style); no separate Base; the Champion is attackable, protected only by units and by staying deep in its realm. **Open:** the Champion's actual signature-ability list, exact AP costs, and how Champion defense/retaliation works (ties to D15) are undesigned — this decision fixes the *shape* of the economy, not its content. → `../champions.md`; resolves win-condition + Base-merge keystones and overview.md §7/§10 opens.

**Movement/realm rule — revised again, tightened same day, under test (2026-08-08).** The "cheap move collapses the network" framing above is superseded. The Champion is now a **hard lock-in** while connected: `2AP` move, restricted to hexes that are **themselves already bonded** (not merely reachable through the network — an earlier same-day draft allowed that and playtesting immediately caught it: "I can still move outside of the network"); to move elsewhere it must first spend `0AP` on the new **Collapse Network** ability, which drops every bond outright (not a reversible pause — they're gone, re-bond from scratch), after which it moves freely at `1AP` like any creature. A Champion that never bonded, or is currently fully blockaded (D8's reversible pause, untouched by this change), already counts as disconnected and so is never rooted. Flagged "under test" pending playtest — may be revisited if the lock proves too punishing or Collapse too free. → `../champions.md` §"The Champion action economy," realm constraint.

**Mana snapshot + pre-bonded start — under test (2026-08-08).** Mana is refreshed **once per Beginning phase only** (`RefreshManaEffect`) — a bond made mid-turn is permanent but its mana isn't credited until the *following* Beginning phase, the same pessimistic "no retroactive unlock this turn" shape D14 already uses for summoning sickness. Paired with this: **both Champions now start match setup pre-bonded to their own home tile**, so 1 mana is live from turn 1 instead of needing a live Bond + a full turn cycle first — and, combined with the tightened movement rule above, a fresh Champion is *already rooted* from turn 1. → `../champions.md` §"The Champion action economy."

**Superseded model (kept for history):** originally the Champion had **two independent economies** — mana, and a single **Channel** action per turn spent on exactly one of {bond a terrain, act as a creature (move/attack), activate a Champion ability}, with bond-vs-activate as the central tension. Replaced above because a Champion-only "Channel" resource duplicated the AP model creatures already use (pillar 5) — the Champion is now simply a special creature, not a third combat model.

### D8 — Resource system: terrain deck connected outward from the Champion
Separate terrain deck (size varies by format), laid out randomly in the home base at start. Up to once/turn, connect one terrain reachable from the Champion via already-connected, unblocked terrains — a spatial, deterministic ramp that eliminates draw-based mana/color screw. 8 colors (8 unrestricted basics + restricted non-basics). The **Champion is the network root** (answers "is the Champion the resource engine?" → yes). Denial is positional ("put the connection on hold"), reversible, and counterable. **Severing rule RESOLVED to (a) pause:** a Champion draws mana from a bonded terrain only while a path of enemy-free terrain connects it back to the Champion; an enemy on any path node **pauses** downstream mana reversibly (kill/reroute to restore). Bonding itself is permanent — only the *draw* is conditional. → `../resources-terrain.md`. **M1 debug-UI implementation note (2026-08-08, scoped to the current placeholder board, not a reversal of the terrain-deck/8-color vision above):** M1's board has no terrain deck yet — every scenario just marks a few hexes as "terrain" so `CanBondTo` had something to gate on, which read as an arbitrary, unexplained restriction once rendered (green hexes with no in-game meaning). Two simplifications for the current build only: (1) **any** hex is bondable — the terrain-type gate is dropped until a real terrain deck exists to make that distinction meaningful; (2) **before a Champion has bonded anything, the only legal first bond is its own current tile** — the network must root at home before spreading outward (previously the first bond could be any adjacent cell). Consequence worth flagging: `Query.ResolveConnectedProducingTerrain`'s BFS only ever tested a root's *neighbors*, so a Champion standing on its own bonded tile wouldn't have counted as connected to it — fixed alongside this change (`Query.IsBondedAndReachable`) so the home-tile bond produces mana immediately. → `src/Leyline.RulesCore/Queries/Query.cs`.

### D7 — Default visibility is full
Board is fully visible by default; all concealment (Mist/Submerged/Mimic) is opt-in via cards. Simplest default that still exposes the perception hook. → `../asymmetric-information.md`, `../overview.md` §9.

### D6 — Traps are supported, as a special case of the stack
A trap = a pre-committed, **hidden triggered ability** (pillar 6 hides the pre-commitment) that goes on the stack when it fires. No separate subsystem. → `../interaction-stack.md`.

### D5 — Interaction model: priority + lightweight stack (players can act on the opponent's turn)
The engine has a real priority/stack capability (LIFO, responses-to-responses). Kept tame by: (1) priority only at **defined windows** (something goes on the stack; a Combat is declared); (2) **most default cards are sorcery-speed**, so the stack is invisible to new players. Unifies instants, combat tricks, and traps into one mechanism. **Biggest complexity commitment so far**; accepted because the user wants MTG-style interaction and it feeds the deception pillar. → `../interaction-stack.md`.

### D4 — Board is a container: a hex holds up to 3 ground creatures
A hex is larger than a creature. Up to **3 ground creatures** occupy it (alternating corners; the other 3 corners reserved for a later use — TBD). Attacks target a **hex**; the defender **declares defenders** from creatures on that hex. Enables guarding important creatures. → `../overview.md` §2, §4.

### D3 — Combat runs sequentially now, but stays revertible to a phased model
Default: sequential resolution (declare/resolve one attack at a time), with **defend-once-per-turn** as a tactical primitive. Minor goal: model every fight as an explicit `Combat` object (`{attackers, target hex, declared defenders} → resolve`) so sequential vs. phased is only a **scheduling policy** — a later revert is a scheduler swap, not a rewrite. Simultaneous multi-attacker-on-one-hex is the feature the phased scheduler would unlock. → `../overview.md` §3–4.

### D2 — Champion progression uses level bands (formats by power ceiling)
Power is a **step function**: horizontal (sidegrades) *within* a band, a discrete jump *between* bands. Matches/tournaments are defined per band ("bring a level 1–5 champion"), so players never face a champion outside their band — vertical growth never creates an unfair match. Enables phased launch (ship band 1 first) and three distinct metagames. **Open:** down-leveling (may a higher champion play a lower bracket at a capped config?). → `../champions.md`.

### D1 — Meta/core firewall confirmed
Champion/card progression is **never** match-internal. Leveling happens outside matches, at the player's pace, as a deliberate build decision. The match core consumes a resolved loadout only. → `../champions.md`, PLAN §6.

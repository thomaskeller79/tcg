# Combat scenarios

*Small board situations with a desired outcome, for checking any change to combat or to Activation-Point costs. Play a proposed rule through each scenario and rate it ✓ (desired outcome), – (a matter of preference) or ✗ (fails). Not real cards.*

**Defaults unless a scenario says otherwise:** creatures have Move `1AP`, Attack 3 with the default Attack cost, Melee; rules as in `../economy.md`, `../interaction-stack.md` §Combat integration.

---

## Agreed and played through (D116)

Desired outcomes agreed with the user; the six cost options rated against them are in `../history/playtest-variants.md` §Attack and Defend cost. The current rules (Attack `3~AP`, Defend `1~AP`) pass S1, S2a, S2c and S3; S2b is a matter of style.

- **S1 — mirror trade.** Two creatures, each 4 AP, Attack 3, Life 5. A's attacks B's, B's defends; in B's turn B's attacks back. **Desired:** a draw (both die) — attacking first must not be worse than being attacked.
- **S2a — reach pays off.** a (5 AP, Attack 3, Life 4) moves 2 and attacks e (Attack 3, Life 3), which defends and dies. **Desired:** the all-in attack is worth it.
- **S2b — all in, the target survives.** As S2a, but e has Life 5 and attacks a in its controller's turn. **Preference:** a can't defend (rewards getting into position first) or a can (rewards attacking) — both legitimate styles.
- **S2c — step adjacent and wait.** a (5 AP) moves next to e (4 AP) without attacking; e attacks, a defends (both now hurt); then a attacks e. **Desired:** the symmetric trade — whoever attacks first must not lose for that reason (no standoff).
- **S3 — Neutral bait.** An idle creature (4 AP, Attack 3, Life 7) is attacked by a Neutral monster (Attack 2, Life 3) in Neutral A's turn, then by the other Champion's creature in that Champion's turn. **Desired:** it can defend against each opponent once.

## Not played through — expected outcomes proposed, not agreed

Proposed by Claude while listing scenarios; never discussed in detail or rated against any option. Agree on the desired outcome before using one.

- **S4 — two attackers on one hex.** In B's turn, two B creatures attack, one after the other, the hex holding A's idle creatures C and D. **Proposed:** C defends one attack and D the other; a single creature can't defend both (without the Defender keyword).
- **S5 — a powerful `!` ability.** A creature uses `2!AP: deal 2 damage to target creature` in its own turn. **Proposed:** it can't defend until its next turn — power costs defence.
- **S6 — AP gain as a trick.** An exhausted creature (as in S5) is attacked; in response, its controller gives it +1 AP with a Reactive card. **Proposed:** it can defend (a surprise defender).
- **S7 — Defender keyword.** An idle creature with Defender (`1AP: Defend`) and 4 AP; its hex is attacked four times in one cycle. **Proposed:** it can defend all four, limited only by its Life.
- **S8 — no multi-attack, no hit-and-run.** A creature with 7 AP attacks, then wants to attack again or move away. **Proposed:** neither is possible in the same turn.

## Settled by a decision, not played through

- **S9 — a spent Champion.** The Champion has 7 AP, uses Draw (5) and Bond (2) in its turn, and is then attacked. Answered by D117: like any creature that spent everything, it can't defend until its next turn.
- **S10 — a Defender fortress.** A hex holding three Defender-keyword creatures, attacked by several creatures one after another. Accepted in D116: attacking it is costly — a reward for whoever sets it up on a hex important enough to need attacking.
